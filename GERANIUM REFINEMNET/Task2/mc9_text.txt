Market Conduct Examination Standards
How an examiner of this Department tests a company's claim files, what the tolerances are, and what
follows from a finding.
Kansas Insurance Department, Market Regulation Division. Bulletin MC-9, in force from July 1, 2024,
superseding MC-8.
1.  Scope
1.1  This bulletin governs every market conduct examination of claims handling the Division conducts on a company
authorized in this state.
1.2  The examiner works from the sample the company produces, the company's own records of what it did with
each file, and the Division's file on the company.
1.3  A working day is a day other than a Saturday, a Sunday or a holiday on the schedule at the back of this bulletin.
A period stated in working days counts neither the day it begins on nor any day excluded by this paragraph.
2.  Scope of the sample
2.1  The sample is drawn from claims of which the company received notice inside the examination period.
2.2  A file the company includes whose notice date falls outside the examination period is removed from the sample.
It is removed from the denominator of every standard as well as from the exceptions, and the examiner states
how many files were removed and why.
2.3  A standard is tested only against the files to which it applies. The denominator of a standard is the number of
files in scope to which that standard applies, and it is not the size of the sample.
2.4  A claim closed without payment is tested under 4.1, 4.2 and 4.4. It is not tested under 4.3 or 4.5, no payment and
no settlement having been made.
3.  How a standard is tested
3.1  The error rate of a standard is the number of exceptions divided by the denominator at 2.3, carried to two
decimal places.
3.2  A standard whose error rate exceeds its tolerance is a finding. A standard whose error rate equals its tolerance is
not.
3.3  The general tolerance is seven percent. The standards at 4.4 and 5.1 carry a tolerance of zero, so a single
exception on either is a finding.
4.  The claim handling standards
4.1  The company acknowledges a claim within ten working days of receiving notice of it.
4.2  The company issues a written decision within thirty calendar days of receiving a completed proof of loss.
4.3  The company pays within ten working days of receiving a signed release.
4.4  A denial states the policy provision the company relies on. Tolerance zero.
4.5  A total loss settlement on a private passenger automobile includes sales tax at seven and one half percent of the
actual cash value and the title and registration fee of 42.50.

5.  Records
5.1  The company maintains a complaint register carrying every complaint the Division has forwarded to it.
Tolerance zero. Each complaint the Division forwarded and the register omits is one exception.
5.2  The register is tested against the Division's own record of what it forwarded, and the Division's record governs.
6.  What follows from a finding
6.1  A penalty is assessed on each finding at the rate in the schedule at the back, multiplied by the number of
exceptions on that standard. The penalty is computed on exceptions and not on the error rate.
6.2  The rate is 250.00 for a finding the company has not been made on before, and 750.00 for a finding on a
standard on which the company was made a finding in its previous examination. The Division's file records
what the previous examination found.
6.3  Where a standard was a finding in the previous examination and is within tolerance in this one, no penalty arises
on it and the examiner records that it has been corrected.
6.4  Where the examination finds that a claimant was paid less than the policy required, the company pays the
difference with interest at ten percent simple from the day it paid to the day of the report. Interest is
computed on a basis of three hundred and sixty five days.
6.5  Restitution under 6.4 is owed whatever the size of the underpayment. A small underpayment is not disregarded.
6.6  The examiner states, for each finding, what the company must do and by when, and states anything the
examination was not able to settle.
